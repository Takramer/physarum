PHYSARUM
A LIVING INTERFACE

ORGANISM

Physarum polycephalum is a single-celled slime mold with no brain and no nervous system, yet it solves mazes, approximates shortest-path networks, and once redesigned the Tokyo rail map more efficiently than its engineers.

What you are watching is 150,000 autonomous agents living in a GPU compute shader. Each one follows three rules: sense, turn, deposit. Everything else (the veins, the pulsing, the hunger) emerges.

BEHAVIOR

The words on this page are food. Agents smell them from across the screen and grow transport veins toward them. Hover any label and the colony reroutes to feed on it.

Click empty space to drop food; the colony swarms the spot. Hold the pointer down to shine light and it scatters (physarum is photophobic). Scroll to shift its color. Your cursor is food too: hold still and it will find you.

SOUND

The audio is not a recording. A generative Web Audio patch — detuned drones, a slow breathing filter, sparse pentatonic plucks through a feedback delay — plays forever without repeating.

The colony's mood leaks into it: moving your cursor opens the filter, feeding the organism strikes new tones, and shining light ducks the drone low until you let go.

SOURCE

Zero frameworks. Zero dependencies. One WebGPU compute pipeline (agents → diffuse → render), one 2D canvas rasterizing the DOM into a pheromone field, one Web Audio graph.
